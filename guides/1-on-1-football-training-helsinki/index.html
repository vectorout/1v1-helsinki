What 1-on-1 training actually is

In a normal team practice, one coach splits attention between 15–20 players. A young player might get thirty seconds of individual feedback in ninety minutes. Private 1-on-1 training flips that ratio: every touch is watched, every rep is corrected, and the whole session is built around one player's specific gaps.

That's why individual training moves players faster than any other format — not because the drills are secret, but because nothing is diluted.

What a good session includes

  • 1v1 mechanics — the feints, shifts of weight and exit moves that actually beat defenders, repeated until they fire without thinking.
  • Explosive speed — first-step acceleration and change of direction: the half metre that decides every duel.
  • Fast footwork under pressure — ball mastery with a defender closing in, not alone in a garage.
  • Honest feedback — a private coach who never tells you what's broken is a paid cheerleader.

What it should not be: jogging through cone slaloms, generic fitness circuits, or drills the player could do alone with a YouTube video.

How to choose a private football coach — the 5-point check

  1. Demand a trial. A coach confident in their standard will show it for free. If there's no trial, ask why.
  2. Ask to see footage. Real training clips with real players — not just a CV. (Ours are on the proof section and on TikTok.)
  3. Ask what the focus is. "Everything" is the wrong answer. A specialist names the thing: duels, speed, finishing.
  4. Watch the feedback. In one session you'll see whether the coach corrects details or just runs drills.
  5. Check retention. Players vote with their feet. Ask how long the current players have stayed.

Who 1-on-1 training is for

Players between 6 and 18, at any club level — from a six-year-old learning their first moves to a teenager pushing for an academy spot. What matters isn't the current level; it's whether the player wants to be the one their team gives the ball to.
